# The Selfish Gene and Cultural Evolution

About Richard Dawkins’s The Selfish Gene. He shows that some species have an altruism gene that determines their behavior, while others do not. A person reading this subconsciously applies the same scheme to humans. It seems to them that people can likewise help one another or refuse to help.

That is the point of the book: to understand how nature determines our desire to help others. But this is exactly where a contradiction appears. A person is determined less by nature, that is, by genes, and more by mentality — by the culture imposed on them. Even if genes say “steal and kill,” a person can still refuse to do it simply because they were raised that way.

That is the first thought. The next thought: on the other hand, history contains the same evolution of cultures. Just as genes evolve on the biological level, culture evolves too. This means that Richard Dawkins’s reasoning about altruistic genes and other animals applies to culture as well. From this point of view, it is very interesting.
