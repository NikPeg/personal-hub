# The Consensus State

I am reading *And Quiet Flows the Don*. Do you know what I like about Bolshevik politics? They knew how to talk. Objectively, at the early stage most people were not on their side, but the “agitators” talked to people, won arguments, and drew them over. That was not dumb propaganda; it was honest heart-to-heart conversation.

In *Andromeda: A Space-Age Tale*, society is shown as one where power belongs not to the majority, as in our world, but to everyone. Decisions are made only unanimously. Society works for the happiness of each person, not the majority. No one is oppressed.

I used to wonder: is that even possible? Could not a single person object? I thought it was a utopia. Now I think there is probably always a solution. Two people, if they talk and think enough, can always reach a consensus, a solution convenient for both, if each thinks about the other and can understand the other’s position.

So imagine a form of government where every decision is accepted by nationwide confirmation, for example in a mobile app or on a website. If even one person is against it, a government representative comes to them or writes to ask why they disagree. They argue and reach a common solution. Maybe that person receives special rights if the issue comes from some personal circumstance. If that person wins the argument, the law might even be passed in the opposite direction. This would produce a state where every person supports government decisions, without the state becoming rigid or inert.
