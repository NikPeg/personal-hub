# A State of Unanimity

I am reading *And Quiet Flows the Don*. You know what I like about Bolshevik politics? They knew how to talk. Objectively, at the initial stage most people were not on their side, but “agitators” spoke with people, won arguments, and persuaded them. This is not dumb propaganda; it is honest heart-to-heart conversation.

In *Andromeda Nebula*, society is shown where power belongs not to the majority, as with us, but to everyone: decisions are made only unanimously. Society works for the happiness of each person, not the majority. No one is oppressed.

I always wondered: is that possible? Could there really be no one against a decision? I thought it was just utopia. But now I think there is probably always a solution. Two people, if they talk and think enough, can always reach consensus, a decision convenient for both, if each thinks about the other and can understand the other’s position.

So imagine a form of government. Every decision is accepted by nationwide confirmation, for example in a mobile app or on a website. If even one person is against it, a government representative visits or messages them to find out why they disagree. They argue and reach a common decision. Maybe that person receives exceptional rights if it is tied to their specific situation. If that person wins the argument, the law may even be adopted in the opposite direction. This would create a state where every person supports state decisions, while the state would not become rigid or sluggish.
