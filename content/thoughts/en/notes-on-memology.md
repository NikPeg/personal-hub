# Notes on Memology

My thoughts on memology: memology, meme theory, memetic theory, thoughts about memology.

I would like to see meme theory developed further. It is obvious that every meme can be described by the following qualities:

- memorability: how long it remains in the carrier’s mind;
- the average number of people with whom the carrier will share this meme; different people have different values;
- locality — a characteristic K. came up with;
- interestingness: the amount of attention a person gives the meme; the time they spend thinking about it; the number of new thoughts created by the meme;
- relevance: some memes lose relevance in days, like a minor news item; some in years, like a change in the political situation; some in millennia, like a language; some are eternal, for example tarot cards may exist as long as the state exists;
- the reaction it evokes: laughter, sadness, reflection, sorrow;
- the purpose of transmission: one person tells a joke to make the listener laugh, another to impress a girl; one person writes a story out of graphomania, another to earn money;
- related memes: when a person remembers something, they may remember or steer the conversation toward a nearby meme;
- competing memes: memes that occupy the same carrier or parasitize the same meme;
- complementary memes: memes that remind a person of the meme under consideration, for example “in short” → “grand Cherokee”;
- complemented memes: memes for which the meme under consideration acts as a complement;
- the carrier of the meme: not all memes are transmitted orally; the spelling of a word, a book, a film — all of these have physical carriers.

The complexity of a meme is a characteristic from Suchkov: the probability that another person will invent exactly the same meme.

Human knowledge of virology can be applied to memes.

Memes can be used to earn money, and mostly they are. For example, “the existence of SmartLearning” is also a meme.

People often transmit memes simply to fill airtime: “Yesterday I heard a news item that...” In other words, they would not tell it if they knew what else to talk about.

My most viable meme is “in short, your army has been defeated.” It actively exists and is still used in the minds of all kinds of people, even people I do not know. And I no longer feed it myself.

Let us call the ideal carrier of a meme a person who spreads it to all acquaintances, to everyone willing to listen. Shamshev is the ideal carrier of infocommunism, Y. is the ideal carrier of the book The Gray House, and my father is the ideal carrier of everything he hears in the news.

Note that a person may be an ideal carrier not for their whole life, but only for a few days — for example, while a song is stuck in their head.

Among other memes, those that contain a direct call for their own spread stand out. These are, of course, religions: preachers and missionaries were honored and became saints. Another example is videos where the author says at the end, “send this video to your friends.” A third example is insane social-media chain texts like, “send this to ten friends or your mother will die.”

Which memes should be considered the same, and which different?

Try applying the Gartner curve to memes. It seems that a meme’s popularity jumps along roughly the same curve.

The game Atomic Heart is a vivid example of successful use of already existing memes. I see old ones everywhere: “died” as “wasted,” the drawn little man as Vault Boy, the refrigerator from Futurama.

“Adaptability” is how easily a meme can be remade for other templates and contexts. The best example, I think, is gachi: it can be inserted almost anywhere. Memes with several variants — audio, video, text — adapt well.

Ease of repeating a meme. For example, an Amogus or a swastika is easy to draw anywhere.
